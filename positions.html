Currently I don’t have any positions open…

If you hold a Ph.D. and wish to move to the Netherlands and carry out research on automated transport, traffic management, and/or transport equity as a Postdoc, please consider applying to the Marie Curie Postdoctoral Fellowship. Send me an email with the subject line [MCPF – hEATlab] to set up a meeting and discuss the possibility of hosting you.

If you are doing a Master’s at TU Delft, you can reach out to me to do your MSc thesis on one of the following topics. Unless noted otherwise, each can be conducted for the MSc Civil Engineering – Transport & Planning track or the MSc Transport Infrastructure and Logistics.

  • Fair for Whom? Horizontal and Vertical Equity in HOT Lane Pricing — High-occupancy toll (HOT) lanes are usually priced for horizontal equity, matching each traveller’s toll to the cost they impose — but this burden falls hardest on travellers with the lowest value of time. This project develops a dynamic HOT lane pricing strategy targeting vertical equity instead, and compares it against an existing horizontal-equity controller on the SR 167 corridor, building on a working lane-choice/Cell Transmission Model simulation framework so the effort goes into the objective function and the comparison. Suits a student comfortable with Python and mathematical optimisation, with an interest in traffic flow theory, traffic control, and/or transport economics.
  • Development and Analysis of a Particle Model for Regional Traffic Interactions — Bathtub (MFD-based) traffic models assume uniform vehicle density and speed across a region. This project develops a more realistic particle (agent-based) model capturing spatial and temporal variations in density and speed, derives its continuum PDE limit, and validates it against traditional bathtub models. Suits a student with an affinity for both theoretical and applied mathematics and traffic engineering.
  • Data-driven Estimation of Urban Travel Demand from Open Data — Investigates whether Origin–Destination matrices can be predicted directly from open data — street network structure and population density — using regression and neural network models, tested across multiple Dutch cities with ODiN and OpenStreetMap data. Requires strong (or improving) Python skills.
  • Integrating Car-sharing in Transport Modelling — With Haskoning. Determines how car-sharing services can be reliably incorporated into existing transport modelling frameworks (demand modelling, mode choice, or network assignment), implemented in Aimsun Next. An internship position at Haskoning (Delft and/or Amersfoort) is available alongside this thesis.
  • Understanding Car-sharing Adoption — Combines qualitative and quantitative methods (surveys and/or interviews) to identify the drivers and barriers behind who uses car-sharing, why, and what holds wider adoption back — insight aimed at improving both model assumptions and real-world service design.
